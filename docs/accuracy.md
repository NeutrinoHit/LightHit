# Accuracy and speed

## What the test suite checks

The tests (`pytest tests src/lighthit/tests`) use synthetic media and
detectors. They check exact identities and tolerances of the numerical
methods, for example:

* table building, cache keys, readout, acceptance contraction (`test_cache*`,
  `test_directional*`, `test_readout`, `test_model_file`);
* the shower route against the per-band basis route: orders 0 and 1 equal,
  order >=2 within 2% charge and 5% bin L1 (`test_shower_route`);
* order 1 against independent quadrature and Monte-Carlo references
  (`test_prompt`, `test_segment_reference`);
* event composition and the laser flash (`test_event`);
* muon building blocks (`test_joint_muon`, `test_universal_primary`, ...).

They do not check the Geant4/Baikal-GVD numbers below.

### Wavelength quadrature of the scattered orders

The RTE tables are built at `KernelConfig.wavelength_nodes` Gauss-Legendre
nodes on the range of the water table, and order 1 of the shower route and of
the muon secondaries integrates over these nodes; order 0 is integrated over
the continuous spectrum. Order >=2 of a shower is compiled in the three node
bands of the band fold, the other nodes entering through the folded kernels;
the muon primary's order 1 has its own rule (`computation.md`, section 3).
Until LightHit
0.3.0a4 there were nine nodes. The measurements of this section (2026-10-18)
were made on the water grid of that time, 350-610 nm in 10.4 nm steps; the
next section gives the change to the corrected grid. The integrand contains `exp(-mu_a(lambda) r)`, and the Baikal
absorption table has a narrow, kinked transparency window (0.042 /m at
495.6 nm) between the nodes 480 and 522 nm (0.0545 and 0.0512 /m). Nine
nodes therefore lose light with distance; the detected Cherenkov spectrum

### Coarse order 1

The former coarse angular average replaced the regular single-scattering
profile by its value at one harmonic-mean angle. The HG profile is nonlinear
on the scale of the coarse pixel, so preserving the mean of the singular
factor alone lost charge. On an independently integrated Gaussian angular
source the loss is 5-28%; a two-node Gauss rule for the measure
`dnu = dP / theta` reduces it to 0.3-1.2%. Its four moments are
`E[1/theta]`, `1`, `E[theta]`, `E[theta^2]` (`_prompt_screen.py`).

A very broad pixel can also contain angular structure that its covariance
cannot represent. The corrected screen therefore retains parent pixels away
from the receiver direction and substitutes their angular children nearby.
Both levels are accumulated from the same deposited rings, without a second
source sampling. Only one level contributes for a given parent-OM pair.
The 2 m spatial grid and the detailed solver are retained. Integrated-charge
batches no longer split by emission time; the charge is time-translation
invariant. There is no event-dependent normalization or clipping.

Addressed checks of 2026-10-22 use the brightest order-1 MC OM in each of the
20-50, 50-100 and 100-150 m bins of the saved run-0 sources. Entries are coarse
charge / detailed charge, before -> after; these are solver comparisons, not
new photon-MC measurements.

| Source | 20-50 m | 50-100 m | 100-150 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| electron 100 GeV | 0.862 -> 0.988 | 0.682 -> 1.009 | 0.936 -> 0.980 |
| pion 100 GeV | 0.906 -> 1.014 | 0.767 -> 1.014 | 0.885 -> 1.001 |
| nu_e CC | 0.877 -> 0.985 | 0.786 -> 1.009 | 0.815 -> 0.983 |

### Addressed distance check after the path-moment order 0

The numerical tolerance of the direct integral is distinct from physical
agreement with the photon Monte Carlo. The five independent direct-charge
controls used for the path-moment change lie at 25–60 m; they alone do not
validate the farther detector.

An addressed check reused the 16 saved MC runs of electron-100GeV-run0.
Both current routes were evaluated on the exact original MC step file,
verified against the registry SHA256. All common physical fields of the
current and original model are bitwise equal; the current model additionally

#### Independent follow-up of order >=2 at 20–50 m

The apparent 9.8% excess above was investigated on these fifteen receivers
without changing the production route (`ge2-near-electron` in the registry).
An independent continuous-spectrum next-event calculation of the original
steps gives the following all-time order >=2 charge:

| Calculation | Charge, pe |
|---|---:|
| Fast route, detailed basis on all fifteen OMs | 0.63913 |
| Independent first-entry reference, 5 million histories | 0.64863 ± 0.00515 |
| Saved photon MC, 16 runs | 0.58234 ± 0.01550 |

The fast/reference ratio is 0.9854 ± 0.0078. Thus this independent check
does not corroborate a systematic +10% error of the fast route. A low
fluctuation of the saved photon sample is the leading explanation of that
ratio; the finite samples do not prove a unique stochastic cause. The error
from summed squared photon weights is 0.01891 pe, larger than the estimate
from the sixteen run totals; the group has about 948 effective weighted hits.

A follow-up uses the saved 1 and 10 TeV electrons with the same primary
vertex, direction, detector and optical data (Geant4 seed 42). The shower
realization changes with energy. Restricting each comparison to the same
fifteen OMs as the 100 GeV group gives:

| Energy | Photon suppression | Photon runs | Fast >=2 / MC | Effective weighted hits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 GeV | 1 | 16 | 1.098 ± 0.036 | 948 |
| 1 TeV | 1 | 8 | 1.013 ± 0.015 | 4847 |
| 10 TeV | 10 | 4 | 0.997 ± 0.020 | 2462 |

Here the errors use the sum of squared photon weights across all runs,
divided by the squared run count for the variance of the mean. They are
one-standard-error estimates and need not agree with a small sample's
run-spread estimate. The fast charges were reevaluated only at zero
frequency, without time bins or changes to production settings. For the
energy-dependent nearest-step 20–50 m groups, which contain sixteen OMs at
1 and 10 TeV, the corresponding ratios are 1.014 ± 0.014 and 0.988 ± 0.019.
The +10% excess is therefore not persistent across these energies.

The NTSim generator's general default is suppression 1000, but
`photon_mc.py` explicitly sets it to 1 unless overridden; the saved run
metadata confirms the values above. Poisson sampling divides the mean
photon count by the suppression factor and compensates each photon weight
by that factor, including its wavelength-dependent Cherenkov factor.
Suppression increases variance without changing the expected charge.
Consequently the 10 TeV sample has less effective information than the
1 TeV sample despite its higher energy. The Geant4 propagator's separate
suppression setting is inactive here because Geant4 optical production is
disabled; the NTSim generator produces the photons from the saved tracks.

The reference samples wavelength continuously, scores the first entry on
the supplied sphere and continues higher collision orders by compensated
roulette, with no finite order cutoff. Its first-order charge is
1.3982 ± 0.0083 pe, consistent with the saved MC 1.3940 pe. Independent
analytic tests check its HG-cap probability, first-collision integral and
all-order collision normalization.

Addressed numerical and geometry controls exclude the following as causes
of a 10% group shift:

* replacing the four preview charges by detailed charges changes the group
  by about 0.0004%; the preview charges themselves are less than 0.3% of it;
* halving the source cells changes the group by about 0.0004%; increasing
  the azimuthal band from 4 to 8 changes it by 0.033%; using all 32 physical
  wavelength bands instead of the fold agrees within 4e-8 relative;
* a rebuilt single-band kernel matches its prepared counterpart; doubling
  the k cutoff is negligible at the three checked receivers;
* increasing source degree to 128 and scattering degree to 96 changes the
  brightest OM by about -1.4 to -1.6% in the three node bands. This small
  angular truncation effect remains distinct from the alleged 10% bias;
* 31,353 weighted control rays through the actual NTSim tracer have no
  missing intersections or mislabelled orders. Detector response without
  absorption agrees within 3e-12. With physical absorption and a nonzero
  emission-time offset the relative difference is below 9e-10; entry times
  differ by less than 2e-6 ns. Earlier hits on other OMs remove 0.0067%
  of their weight. Optical coefficients agree to floating-point precision;
* the original MC's finite trajectory length has a conservative missing-tail
  bound below 6.4e-11 pe here, using the detected source yield and maximum
  albedo 0.2447. No source is near the 3 m table boundary and this event has
  no delayed-light groups.

No gain correction, event normalization or slower production calculation
was introduced. This addressed result does not validate all energies,
geometries or the remaining surface-reference change.

  the runs with 1 (8 runs each, same Geant4 event) gives order 0 at 20-50 m
  1.003 +- 0.021 and total 0.998 +- 0.017;

  older LightHit inputs used the stored step length. They differ on 0.05 %
  of the steps and change no event's yield by more than 1e-5. The bridge now
  uses the same maximum as NTSim;
* the quadrature: Gauss order 32 (instead of 8) with relative tolerance
  1e-9 changes the 123.6 pe of these OMs by 1e-8;

  bright OMs (OM 1241 at 14.6 m: 59.2 against 63.5 +- 1.3 pe; OM 1206 at

Timing of order 0: on the OMs within 50 m LightHit's direct light arrives
0.56 ns (1 TeV electron) and 0.69 ns (1 TeV pion) later on average than the
MC's. The MC records a hit where the photon enters the OM sphere, LightHit
at the OM centre; over the cross-section the mean depth is 2/3 of the
radius, (2/3) 0.216 m x n_g / c = 0.66 ns. In 10 ns bins this moves 2-5 %

attenuation reference.

for every charged track; LightHit multiplies by the charge squared. They
differ only for nuclear fragments, mostly below the Cherenkov threshold.
