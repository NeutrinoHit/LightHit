# Accuracy and speed

## What the test suite checks

The tests (`pytest tests src/lighthit/tests`) use synthetic media and
detectors. They check exact identities and tolerances of the numerical
methods, for example:

* table building, cache keys, readout, acceptance contraction (`test_cache*`,
  `test_directional*`, `test_readout`, `test_model_file`);
* the shower route against the per-band basis route: orders 0 and 1 equal,
  order >=2 within 2% charge and 5% bin L1 (`test_shower_route`);
* order 1 against independent quadrature and Monte-Carlo references
  (`test_prompt`, `test_segment_reference`);
* event composition and the laser flash (`test_event`);
* muon building blocks (`test_joint_muon`, `test_universal_primary`, ...).

They do not check the Geant4/Baikal-GVD numbers below.

### Wavelength quadrature of the scattered orders

Orders 1 and >=2 (and the RTE tables) integrate over wavelength with
`KernelConfig.wavelength_nodes` Gauss-Legendre nodes on 350-610 nm; order 0 is
integrated over the continuous spectrum. Until LightHit 0.3.0a4 there were
nine nodes. The integrand contains `exp(-mu_a(lambda) r)`, and the Baikal
absorption table has a narrow, kinked transparency window (0.042 /m at
495.6 nm) between the nodes 480 and 522 nm (0.0545 and 0.0512 /m). Nine
nodes therefore lose light with distance; the detected Cherenkov spectrum
after r metres of attenuation, relative to a fine trapezoid on the bundle
model (registry `wavelength-quadrature/spectral-integral`):

### Order 0 near emitters: the OM sphere

The direct light of a short step is a thin conical shell; a point receiver at
the OM centre samples it, the OM sphere (radius 0.216 m) averages it. Order 0
uses the sphere for steps within 20 m of the OM and the centre beyond
(`transport-kernel.md`, Direct light on the OM sphere). On the 6 GeV muon
passing OM 267 at 1.76 m, against the NTSim photon Monte Carlo of the same
Geant4 event (31 runs, hits without scattering), registry case
`finite-receiver-order0/muon-near-om-mc`:

| OM (nearest step) | point receiver | sphere within 20 m | sphere everywhere | photon MC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 266 (6.2 m) | 0.020 | 0.211 | 0.211 | 0.197 +- 0.018 |
| 267 (1.76 m) | 63.60 | 59.70 | 59.70 | 60.16 +- 0.50 |
| 268 (10.8 m) | 1.402 | 0.613 | 0.608 | 0.525 +- 0.036 |
| 269 (25.8 m) | 0.048 | 0.048 | 0.031 | 0.032 +- 0.006 |

At OM 267 the point receiver had 39.2 pe from one 8.5 cm muon step whose
shell passes the centre and 16.5 pe from one delta-electron step at 3.6 m
(about 2 pe on the sphere). The Monte Carlo is NTSim's with the photon yield
per true Geant4 step length (`full-rte` d5993d8). Before that fix NTSim's
`CherenkovGenerator` emitted along the chord between the step points, which
removed about 7 % of the light of showers (e+- below 5 MeV, whose true path is
1.2-1.65 times the chord in Geant4's default physics list) and 3.5-6.6 % of
muons; at OM 267 it gave 58.10 +- 0.49 pe. One difference remains by design:

* **Attenuation reference.** The Monte Carlo attenuates each photon to its
  entry point on the glass sphere, LightHit to the centre plane (the reference
  of the effective area). The sphere integral with entry-point attenuation is
  1.5 % higher at OM 267 (60.54 against 59.65 pe); for a plane wave the factor
  is about `exp(mu_ext 2R/3)`, 1.0-1.4 % at any distance. It is within the
  Monte-Carlo error of this comparison.

Beyond 20 m the point receiver stays. Its order-0 error against the sphere
everywhere, per OM with at least 1e-3 pe (cases `finite-receiver-order0/far-*`):

| Input | OMs 20-50 m: median / 90 % / max | OMs 50-100 m: median / 90 % / max | sum point / sphere |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 GeV e (bundle) | 3.7 / 7.2 / 24 % | 3.2 / 8.9 / 20 % | 2.638 / 2.636 pe |
| 100 GeV pi+ | 8.3 / 23 / 46 % | 7.0 / 20 / 46 % | 1.869 / 1.898 pe |
| nu_e CC | 8.4 / 44 / 98 % | 5.5 / 19 / 40 % | 2.277 / 2.368 pe |
| 100 GeV muon | 11 / 18 / 20 % | 6.7 / 16 / 18 % | 0.861 / 0.836 pe |

Order 0 is 40-50 % of an OM's charge at 20-100 m, so this is 1-4 % of the
OM's total charge (median), the sum over the event within 4 %. The error is
largest on dim OMs reached by few steps; it is not a bias. The sphere
everywhere (`finite_receiver_m=inf`) costs 10-14 times the order-0 time; within
20 m the default routes keep their time (100 GeV electron 13.8 s, 1 TeV muon
51.6 s at one thread).

## Reported during development (LightHit 0.3.0a1, not reproduced)

NTSim/Geant4 events in five Baikal-GVD clusters (1440 OMs), one CPU thread,
after Numba compilation. Errors are relative to calculations on the original
Geant4 steps or to a finer source basis, not to photon Monte Carlo.

| Event | Time |
|---|---:|
| electron 100 GeV | 5.5-6.4 s |
| electron 1 TeV | 42 s |
| pi+ 100 GeV | 21 s |
| nu_e CC (e 70 + pi 25 + p 5 GeV) | 14 s |
| muon 100 GeV / 1 TeV | 11 / 10 s |
| nu_mu CC (mu 800 + pi 150 + p 50 GeV) | 73 s |

* Order 0 is exact on the Geant4 steps over the continuous spectrum. A
  nine-wavelength quadrature underestimated it by about 9% for showers (up to
  100% on single OMs).
* Order 1 (grouped, fused bands) against original-step integration: bin L1 at
  most 3.3% (100 GeV) and 3.1% (1 TeV), charge bias below 0.6%. This is the
  largest numerical error of the shower calculation.
* Order >=2 against a finer basis: at most 0.15% bin L1 on 100 GeV electrons,
  0.4% at 1 TeV, 1% on a hadronic shower.
* Hadronic showers emit about 3% of their light 50 ns to milliseconds late;
  that light is read on its own shifted clock or counted in the charge only.

## Reproducible comparison with photon Monte Carlo

`examples/LightHit/lighthit-in-ntsim.ipynb` in NTSim (section 2) runs one
Geant4 event (100 GeV electron) through `LightHitTransport` and through
NTSim's `CherenkovGenerator` -> `MCPhotonPropagator` (up to 30 scatterings,
full photon statistics) -> `SmartRayTracer`; it is the event of
`wavelength-quadrature/mc-electron-100GeV-*`. With 32 wavelength nodes
LightHit gives 6.08 pe (nine nodes: 5.89 pe) in a few seconds; the Monte
Carlo of the notebook run gave 6.56 +- 0.25 pe in about 5 minutes and the
registry's run of the same event 6.26 +- 0.24 pe, together 6.41 +- 0.17 pe
(LightHit/MC 0.95 +- 0.03, of which 1-1.5 % is the attenuation reference).
The brightest OM, 13206: LightHit 1.58 pe, Monte Carlo 1.97 +- 0.15 pe in
the notebook run and 1.61 +- 0.13 pe in the registry run. Single Monte-Carlo
runs scatter by their quoted errors; LightHit returns the same numbers every
time.
